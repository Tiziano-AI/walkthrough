# Direct the walkthrough

## Let the viewer learn the product through use

Organize around the viewer's growing understanding of the product: orient them,
demonstrate a capability through real interaction, show its response, and connect
it to the next relevant capability. Choose a concrete example for continuity.
Its conclusion is not the subject unless the creator asked for that conclusion.
Keep intervening steps when omission would hide a dependency. Do not impose a
fixed chapter template or cover every control.

Revisit a central interactive view when another meaningful action reveals how
it works. For a spatial explorer, orbiting to reveal a cluster, selecting a
point and seeing it in linked views are different demonstrations of one
capability. One decorative rotation before a long analytical report is not.
For a document workflow, the defining interaction may instead be moving from a
finding to its supporting passage. Existing data can support either journey
without recreating intake or computation.

## Select scenes that reveal capabilities

Choose scenes for what they teach about the product. An example finding earns
its place when it reveals an interaction, relationship or useful response.
Prefer a second interaction that changes the viewer's understanding over a
second example that repeats it. Selection can reveal a connection between views;
constraints can then show how that same connection responds to a new question.
Record actual behavior before writing its explanation, and preserve the
conditions that change what the viewer understands. Distinguish what the user
changed from what the app reported.

For a narrated transformation, compare the subject at the selected clip's start
and end. A pointer crossing a chart while its plotted range stays unchanged
does not demonstrate panning. Even if your action notes say “pan the chart,”
those notes cannot supply the missing change. Select another recorded successful
interaction, explain the behavior actually shown, or acquire a missing view
within the mandate. Do not keep an unsupported claim merely because its voice
has already been synthesized.

When an export is the payoff, show the actual usable file. A download button
proves only a control exists. Conversely, an interactive result does not need
an invented export requirement.

## Give attention a coherent path

Compose the viewer's changing understanding, not an animation for each action.
The overview establishes where the subject belongs. A closer view makes a
specific relationship readable. The gesture changes it; the response gives the
viewer a reason to continue. Re-establish context after a cut changes place or
object. Approach a detail from an established view, then settle the camera for
the consequential gesture and response. Move back when the next capability
needs that wider context. A direct cut can be clearer than a pan between
unrelated subjects; smoothness alone does not motivate a move. Do not chase the
pointer or zoom at every click. The pointer makes input legible; camera and
emphasis direct attention to what the product does with that input.

Use a brief outline when the viewer needs to locate one part of an otherwise
readable view, or a restrained spotlight when surrounding information competes
with the subject. Keep enough surroundings to understand the relationship and
remove the treatment when its job is done. A naturally prominent change may
need neither. Emphasis is not the application's selection state: do not imply
that the product itself highlighted something it did not.

The public edit supports timed `picture.emphasis` using observed mark targets
or source rectangles. Its geometry stays fixed on that source; it is not object
tracking. Frame a region containing the relevant movement, or emphasize the
settled response afterward, rather than draw a stale box around the object's
starting position. Camera transforms keep the emphasis aligned; they do not
make its semantic target current. Choose timing against the real response and
check captions against both the subject and treatment. Read public edit help
for the exact input shape instead of copying a remembered grammar.

The product owns the full 1920×1080 frame. Check at delivery size: scaling and
encoding can ruin readable source text. Preserve context in the overview and use detail
framing to reveal the subject, not to create constant movement. Captions must
remain available in the delivered viewing experience, not disappear as a cure
for bad placement. The runtime renders restrained phrase captions over the
full-frame product, at most two visible lines, and supplies complete portable
VTT. Choose placement against the actual shot: keep the control, response and
evidence being discussed clear.
Avoid a permanent panel and duplicate player captions. When narration produces
dense text, edit the speech and shot together rather than making the interface
smaller.

## Compose motion, reading and speech

Live source stays at 1×. Cut uninformative waiting honestly while retaining the
states needed to understand the transition. A settled reading hold gives time
to read; pending work stays live. Nominal 60 fps output is not evidence of
smooth source motion. Keep detail zoom at or below 1.5× from the qualified
3200×1800 source; above that, find a better retained view rather than enlarging
unreadable text.

Use installed edit help for the source-set identity and digest, picture ranges,
settled-observation references, framing, caption placement and independent
narration inputs. The edit names the exact sealed sources. Picture selections
determine the film's length; each spoken thought has its own position on the
output timeline and may cross picture boundaries. Do not make picture length a
function of synthesized speech. Validation can check structure and source
ranges before synthesis, but actual voice durations determine speech fit.

Place each thought against the moment it helps: a reason can precede the
gesture, while interpretation can arrive with the visible response. Starting
every sentence at a shot boundary and padding its end creates a repeated
talk-then-stop rhythm. Let related actions continue across narration boundaries;
split a long explanation where a new response changes what there is to say.

Silence gives the viewer the next part of the work: follow a rotation, notice
what changed, read a newly revealed passage or compare visible values. The
subject need not keep moving, but the viewer should still have something to
learn from looking. Once that task is complete, develop it through the next
action or leave the shot. Pointer motion, decorative zooms and long narration
padding do not supply a task. Do not compensate for an inert gap by narrating
continuously either; relocate the thought or cut the unused interval.

Use measured speech gaps to find passages to inspect, including gaps crossing
a picture boundary. Read the surrounding thoughts and actual footage before
choosing a repair. Recorded actions or camera changes say what occurred, not
whether it was interesting or comprehensible. A stationary comparison can be
useful despite having no events. Choose speech and holds for comprehension,
not an arbitrary duration. If speech overruns, shorten or move the thought,
carry it over a meaningful next shot or select other useful retained footage.
Do not manufacture a hold solely to fit it, accelerate speech or freeze an
unfinished response. A smooth camera cannot repair jerky source; recapture
cannot repair a sentence.

Explain what the viewer can do, why the capability is useful and what the
visible response means. “Select a point and the same model lights up in the
projections” connects action and response. Then let the viewer watch that
happen. “This is the best model for this workload” shifts the subject to advice
about the example. “I click Add to shortlist” merely recites a control.

Use short complete thoughts, specific names where useful and silence while the
viewer explores a relationship or reads. State a limitation once where it
changes interpretation or use: “These models have no cost measurement” explains
a visible state. Unsolicited “not an official recommendation” disclaimers,
validation recitals and provenance belong neither in this script nor over the
product. Technical evidence stays in the companion files.

Use the operator's saved default or the creator's requested provisioned voice,
and reuse unchanged compatible speech. Set the chosen
language explicitly. Canonical text owns captions. Alignment estimates timing;
ASR separately recognizes words and exposes lexical differences. Neither
establishes natural pronunciation, emphasis or transitions: listen for those
and for omitted or repeated words. Check a flagged phrase before deciding
whether the speech or the recognizer is wrong. Do not regenerate automatically.
When a thought crosses a cut, inspect caption placement on both pictures.

## Construct one connected passage

This illustrative example introduces a workspace through moving a connected
object and then inspecting it. It is a construction model, not a report of a
recorded occurrence or a recipe for a particular site. The full public edit
example shows how to express picture ranges, camera, emphasis and independent
narration; take syntax from installed help. Replace illustrative anchors with
the inspected times and marks from your own sealed source.

### Start with the source moments

Suppose inspection of `source-direction` establishes these moments:

| Source moment | What must be visible | Editorial use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `subject-before` | Two connected objects and an unchanged neighboring landmark | Recognize the object and its relationship before input |
| Gesture begins through pointer-up | The same object actually moves relative to that landmark; the connection responds | Show the defining capability, not merely a moving pointer |
| `connection-response` | The changed layout and connection remain readable | Let the viewer recognize what the product preserved |
| `next-capability` | Opening the moved object's details reveals its contents | Develop the same object rather than introduce an unrelated demo |

These labels locate useful evidence. A mark alone does not prove movement or
settlement. Inspect the start and end of the gesture; select only the range
whose actual behavior supports the explanation. Use a settled hold only when
its retained observation permits one. A response that is still changing stays
live. A missing consequence changes the passage you can author.

### Build picture and speech around those moments

| Edit section | Picture construction | Speech and attention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `orientation` | Include the recognizable starting view; approach the subject once before input begins | Start “Arrange the workspace without losing the connections.” while both objects and the relationship are visible |
| `gesture` | Continue the actual drag at 1× in the settled detail framing | Let the thought cross into the drag instead of finishing speech and then starting input; keep the moving object and its connector visible |
| `response` | Retain enough of the changed layout to recognize its relationship | Briefly outline the connection-response region only if the consequence is hard to locate; leave a quiet moment to register it, not an end-of-sentence allowance |
| `next` | Carry the same subject into its actual detail opening; use a cut if source waiting adds nothing | Begin “Open the same object to work on its contents.” across the transition; widen or reframe only where the newly revealed contents require it |

Do not automatically cut at these labels: `orientation`, `gesture` and
`response` can be adjacent ranges of uninterrupted footage. Preserve source
and framing continuity when they are. Place narration on the output clock,
not a sentence-per-picture clock. Synthesized durations then tell you where
speech really ends; they do not extend the pictures. If the first thought
finishes earlier than expected, keep only the quiet interval in which the
viewer is still following the gesture or recognizing its consequence.

An emphasis target is bound to its picture's source. For the moving part,
choose a region that contains the demonstrated relationship; for the settled
response, use its observed response geometry. Keep it brief and out of the
caption path. A framing mark does not authorize a frozen reading hold.

### Compare the visible results

**Flat edit:** A toolbar close-up appears without the two objects. “Move an
object” finishes, then the drag begins. The picture ends at pointer-up. A long
stationary shot follows, and a jump into a details pane starts “Open details.”
The viewer sees controls being used but must reconstruct what moved, what was
preserved and why the next pane belongs to the same operation.

**Directed edit:** The overview identifies the object and connection. The
approach gives that relationship one readable framing and settles before
input. Speech leads into the drag. The object moves against the unchanged
landmark and the connection responds. The brief quiet response lets the viewer
recognize this consequence, with restrained emphasis only if needed. Speech
then carries the same object into its contents. The capabilities feel linked
because the subject and the viewer's understanding persist across them—not
because every shot has an animation.

Preview this connected passage with measured speech before extending the
treatment across the film. Compare the same source and wording, not a new
application occurrence. If the source cannot show the claimed transformation,
camera and narration cannot rescue it.

## Cases where more movement is the wrong repair

**Stationary comparison:** Two values are newly revealed and both are readable
in one view. A quiet stationary shot lets the viewer compare them. A pan between
them or alternating spotlights can make comparison harder by hiding one while
showing the other. Leave them together; continue when their difference is clear.
If neither value is new and there is no comparison to make, keeping the same
screen longer does not become useful merely because it is called a reading hold.

**Unrelated next subject:** A search result beside its source pane deserves one
stable detail view as selections reveal different locations. An export dialog
elsewhere is a new subject; a direct, oriented cut may explain that transition
better than a tour across the whole interface. Do not zoom toward each click
and return by habit.

**Delayed result:** The product is still loading. Keep meaningful genuine live
waiting or cut honestly to the completed state. Do not freeze its spinner,
outline an expected result or make a decorative camera move pretend something
has happened. A cut removes waiting; it does not manufacture a faster response.

## Iterate on the relationship, not the whole production

Use `validate` for source ranges, edit structure, emphasis geometry and
structurally invalid caption placement. `speechTiming:"requires-audio"` leaves
speech fit unchecked. Use preview/composition results and retained narration-fit
diagnostics for measured overlaps and audio-dependent caption fit. Inspect the
measured gaps with their surrounding speech and picture activity. Editorial
observations, such as a camera move during input, invite judgment rather than
block a film automatically. Use retained marks for framing and emphasis and
inspect the resulting shot; a box is not semantic tracking.

Preview the interval that can settle the live question with the same renderer.
Include enough before and after a cut to judge continuity; an isolated target
frame cannot show whether the move makes sense. `--reuse-audio` uses a retained
composition's compatible WAVs and alignment, including speech crossing the
range boundary. Changing a picture or caption needs no new speech. A changed
sentence needs its own measured timing before you can judge fit.

Inspect and watch the current successful preview; a failed replacement keeps
it available and has its own inspectable identity. Previews preserve source
gain, so judge final loudness on the composed delivery. Do not manufacture
additional full compositions, decoded-frame collections or duplicate masters
to answer one local editing question.

## Judge the finished experience

Follow the film without mentally adding source-code knowledge. Can a viewer
explain what the product is for, recognize its main working areas and understand
its defining interaction? Would they know how to begin exploring it?
Inspect opening, transitions, dense evidence, final result and full playback.
Posters and contact sheets cannot reveal stutter or exhausting rhythm. Preserve
unavailable review as a limitation, never as a technical proxy for acceptance.
